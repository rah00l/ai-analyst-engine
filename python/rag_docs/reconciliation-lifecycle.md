# Reconciliation Lifecycle

## What Is Payment Reconciliation

Payment reconciliation is the process of verifying that we have been paid correctly for all transactions that affiliate networks claim to have paid us for. When a consumer makes a purchase through one of our partner programs, the affiliate network reports that transaction to us and eventually sends payment. Reconciliation is how we confirm that the payment we received matches what was reported, close the transaction in our system, and trigger the downstream calculations that determine how much we owe our partners and consumers. Without reconciliation, we cannot pay our partners their share of commission, because the system requires confirmed payment before calculating distributions.

## Invoice Date Versus Payment Date

Affiliate networks track two important dates for every payment cycle. The first is the invoice date (or commission reported date), which is when the merchants pay the network for the transactions. The second is the payment date, which is when the network subsequently pays us. These two dates can be weeks or even months apart. It is critical to understand that reconciliation happens based on the payment date — the date money actually arrives in our bank account — not the invoice date. The payment date becomes the deposit date entered during the reconciliation process, and it determines which accounting period the reconciled transactions fall into.

## The Five Step Process

Payment reconciliation follows five main steps in sequence. First, new payments are identified — either manually by logging into each network's interface or automatically via the payment history web scraper. Second, the accounting team confirms with the finance department that the expected deposit amount has actually been received in the correct bank account. Third, the payment file is obtained from the affiliate network — this is the file listing every transaction included in the payment. Fourth, the payment file is parsed and validated, checking for errors, unknown bonuses, or unexpected data. Fifth, if parsing completed cleanly, the file is reconciled — transactions are closed in the database, commissions are calculated, and the deposit is recorded.

## Finding New Payments

Historically, finding new payments required the reconciliation team to manually log into each affiliate network's web interface every week, check for new payment notifications, cross-reference them against what had already been reconciled using the deposit report in the admin tool, and manually record dates, amounts, and payment IDs in spreadsheets. This process has been largely automated with a payment history web scraper that logs into network accounts automatically, pulls payment history, matches it against the database to identify what has and has not been reconciled, and generates a DepositsToVerify spreadsheet listing everything that needs attention next.

## Confirming Deposits With Accounting

Before reconciliation can begin on any payment, someone in the accounting team with access to bank statements must confirm that the money has actually arrived. This is a critical control step — it prevents reconciling against a payment the network says they sent but that has not yet been received or was sent to the wrong account. The confirmation process involves reviewing the DepositsToVerify spreadsheet generated by the scraper, checking each entry against the bank statement, and marking confirmed deposits. Only after a deposit is confirmed can the reconciliation team proceed with parsing and loading the corresponding payment file.

## What Happens After Reconciliation

Once a payment file is fully reconciled, several downstream processes are triggered automatically. Matched transactions are marked as closed in the database, which means they are no longer outstanding. Partner commission calculations begin — the system determines each partner's share based on the agreed revenue-sharing ratios. Consumer cashback or points calculations are also triggered where applicable. The reconciled data becomes available in accounting reports, including the deposit report, reconciled transaction summary, and partner commission payment reports. The payment file itself moves to FULL RECONCILED status and requires no further action.
